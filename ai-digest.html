Where Reliability Lives in Vision-Language Models: A Mechanistic Study of Attention, Hidden States, and Causal Circuits

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Sharp attention maps do NOT reliably predict confident or calibrated VLM answers — the intuitive 'attention-confidence assumption' is empirically falsified.
  • Hidden states and causal circuits, not attention weight distributions, are the more reliable indicators of model trustworthiness.
  • Has direct implications for interpretability tools that rely on attention visualization as a proxy for model certainty.

On Distinguishing Capability Elicitation from Capability Creation in Post-Training: A Free-Energy Perspective

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Challenges the common framing that SFT = imitation and RL = discovery — the real distinction is whether training increases the probability of outputs the base model could never produce.
  • Applies a free-energy lens to post-training: elicitation reshapes the distribution over existing capabilities, while creation genuinely expands it.
  • Practical consequence: knowing which regime you're in determines whether more RL will yield genuinely new behaviors or just surface latent ones.

Domain-level metacognitive monitoring in frontier LLMs: A 33-model atlas

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Tested 33 frontier LLMs across 1,500 MMLU items in six domains — aggregate metacognition scores mask large within-model variation by domain.
  • Models that appear well-calibrated overall can be significantly overconfident or underconfident in specific knowledge areas.
  • Provides a practical atlas for identifying which model families have the worst domain-specific calibration gaps.
