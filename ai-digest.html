Do Synthetic Personas Predict Real Audience Response? A Sim-to-Real Study Where a No-Persona Baseline Beats Persona-Based Copy Simulation

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Marketers increasingly use LLMs as “synthetic personas” to predict how an audience will react to copy before it ships.
  • A sim-to-real comparison finds a no-persona baseline actually outperforms persona-conditioned copy simulation at predicting real audience response.
  • Challenges the assumption that profile-conditioned LLMs reliably mimic real human sample reactions — a caution for AI-driven market research.

Ovis-Embedding: Pushing the Frontiers of Universal Omni-Modal Embeddings

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Ovis-Embedding is a new omni-modal embedding family covering text, image, video, and audio in a single model.
  • Uses a shared multimodal backbone instead of bolting together separate per-modality towers, aiming for more unified cross-modal representations.

What Does 99% Accuracy Measure? A Reproducible Audit of Shortcut Learning in a Widely Used Fake News Corpus

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Classifiers trained on the widely-used ISOT/Kaggle “Fake and Real News” dataset routinely score above 98% accuracy — suspiciously high given how hard veracity assessment actually is.
  • A transparent TF-IDF plus linear-classifier audit shows this performance is largely driven by shortcut learning on dataset artifacts, not genuine fake-news detection.
  • A cautionary case study for anyone citing high benchmark accuracy as evidence of real-world capability.

Same Quantity, Different Answer: Numerical Representation Invariance in Language Models

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Tests whether LLMs give the same answer to numerically-equivalent word problems when the quantity is written differently (decimal, fraction, percent, number word, scientific notation).
  • Built a 3,600-problem exact-rational benchmark specifically to probe this representation invariance.
  • Findings bear directly on how much to trust LLM math/reasoning outputs when a number’s surface form changes but its underlying value doesn’t.
