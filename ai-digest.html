Research & Papers

The Window Is in the Reasoning: When LLM Agents Decide to Follow Prompt Injections

ArXiv cs.CL
  • LLM agents that read untrusted content can be hijacked by prompt injections.
  • Runtime monitors that try to catch a hijack before the agent acts, and benchmarks that score each task with a single run, share an assumption about whether an agent follows an injection.
  • The title points to the agent's reasoning as the window in which the decision to follow an injection is made.

Doing Less with More: A First-Principles Exploration of the Suitability of Agentic Computing over Alternative Architectural Choices

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Interest is growing in automating business activities with agentic AI because of its seeming ease of use.
  • The authors note it has never been easier, or costlier, to do less with more.
  • The paper examines from first principles when agents are preferable to alternative architectures.

ArXiv cs.AI

Translating in style: An open-weight alternative to GPT-4 for culturally aligned machine translation

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Machine translation often preserves meaning but flattens linguistic style, most acutely when translating into non-Western languages.
  • Builds on the RASTA framework of Havaldar et al. (2025a), which addresses this through retrieval-augmented approaches.
  • Offers an open-weight alternative to GPT-4 for culturally aligned translation.
