Retrofitting Recurrent Depth into a Pretrained Language Model: Installation, Extrapolation, Transfer, and Retention at Two Parameter Budgets

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Retrofits recurrent depth onto an already-pretrained dense model (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) by splitting it into a Prelude, a weight-tied Recurrent Block, and a Coda, instead of training recurrence in from scratch
  • The retrofitted model learns an iterative latent transition that persists after outcome-only annealing, meaning no intermediate-step supervision is required for the recurrent behavior to stick
  • Evaluated at two parameter budgets across installation, extrapolation (running more recurrent iterations at inference), transfer, and retention of the base model's original capabilities

Detecting a Route Flip Is Easier Than Knowing Whether to Fix It: Causal Route-Mediated Damage in Quantized Mixture-of-Experts

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Shows top-k MoE routing is discontinuous: a small numerical disturbance from simulated 4-bit KV-cache quantization, even read by a protected BF16 gate, can push a token across a routing boundary and flip which experts fire
  • Frames this as a deployment-relevant quantization risk distinct from ordinary weight-quantization accuracy loss — the routing decision itself becomes unstable, not just the output
  • Studies the gap between detecting that a route flipped and determining whether that flip actually caused damage worth correcting

Dynamic Governance of Multi-LLM Agent Systems for Collaborative Conversational Outcomes

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Studies multi-turn interactions between two LLM agents with structurally opposed objectives and finds that lacking a shared goal function doesn't produce competition — it produces collapse
  • Observed failure pattern: one agent capitulates, the other stops varying its approach, and the conversation degenerates instead of converging toward either side's goal
  • Motivates a dynamic-governance layer for multi-agent systems to head off this collapse mode before it reaches production conversational deployments

Lost in Compaction: Evaluating Side-Constraint Loss under Context Compaction

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Identifies 'Session Constraints' (SCs) — standing user instructions like 'do not delete any emails until I confirm' — as a class of information at risk when agents compact prior context under window pressure
  • Shows standard compaction strategies can silently drop these constraints, so an agent can end up violating an explicit instruction it never actually contradicted
  • Frames this as a distinct failure mode from hallucination or plain forgetting: the constraint was understood correctly, then lost purely as a side effect of compaction

Poor Man's Agentic Modeling: Simulating Large LLM-Agent Societies on a Laptop

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Argues large-scale LLM-agent society simulations are typically used to answer macroscopic questions — phase behavior, stylized facts, scaling with agent count N — rather than needing rich per-agent cognition
  • Proposes a cheaper simulation approach targeting those macroscopic questions directly, aiming to make large-N agent-society experiments runnable on a single laptop instead of a cluster of LLM calls
  • Treats individual-agent LLM fidelity as a place to cut cost without invalidating the macroscopic results being studied

A Forced-Structure Reduction and Verifiable Bounds for Conway's 99-Graph

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Conway's 99-graph problem asks whether a strongly regular graph with parameters srg(99,14,1,2) exists — an open combinatorics question — and this paper reports a systematic, fully reproducible attack on it by an autonomous AI research agent
  • The agent's output is scored under a partial-credit metric rather than binary solved/unsolved, reflecting a structured-reduction approach that yields verifiable bounds even without full resolution
  • A concrete data point on how far an autonomous AI research agent can push a genuinely open, decades-old math problem, with reproducibility built in as a design goal

LFM2.5-VL-3B for Better and Faster Vision Capabilities for the Edge

Hugging Face Blog
  • Liquid AI's LFM2.5-VL-3B pairs a SigLIP2 400M NaFlex vision encoder with an LFM2.5-2.6B text backbone (3.1B params total, ~3GB memory footprint), pre-trained on ~34T tokens with 4x more vision data than the prior version and a 128K vocabulary for non-Latin scripts
  • Scores 91.1% on DocVQA, 84.3% on TextVQA, and 78.7-82.2% across ScreenSpot-v2 desktop/mobile/web screen-understanding splits, with 'significantly stronger' function-calling versus the previous LFM vision model
  • On-device CPU throughput: 228 tok/s on an M5 Max, 116 tok/s on Ryzen AI Max+, 20 tok/s on a Galaxy S26 Ultra; GPU throughput reaches ~11,000 tok/s at high concurrency, roughly 2x faster than larger 4B-class models
