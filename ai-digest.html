AI Morning Digest

Today's research leans heavily into agentic AI architecture and evaluation: papers tackle bounded-context long-horizon reasoning, bias-robust LLM-as-judge auditing, and rethinking how agents should learn from experience instead of relying on simple trajectory reflection. Several papers also push LLMs into doing science itself, from benchmarking AI Scientist output quality to a pipeline for generating and validating mathematical conjectures. On the practical side, Simon Willison shipped a 1.0 release of his condense-json library.

Research & Papers

OpenClaw and Ollama in Agentic AI: Toward Fully Autonomous and Scalable AI Agent Systems

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Argues the shift from reactive LLMs to persistent, action-capable agents has exposed architectural gaps around cleanly separating inference, orchestration, and execution layers.
  • Uses OpenClaw and Ollama as case studies for building toward fully autonomous, scalable agent systems.

Can AI Evaluate AI Scientists? A Benchmarking Study of Autonomous Research Generation Systems Using Automated Multi-Model Review

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Tackles the open problem of evaluating and comparing the quality of papers produced by autonomous 'AI Scientist' research systems.
  • Proposes a rigorous benchmarking method built on automated multi-model review rather than a single evaluator model.

LLM Framework for Discovering Major Mathematical Conjectures: AI's Quest for the Next Riemann Hypothesis

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Presents a three-stage pipeline for systematically generating and validating mathematical conjectures, an area that has historically depended on expert intuition.
  • Aims at conjecture generation with 'substantial mathematical potential' rather than routine theorem-proving.

ThinkReset: Learnable Intermediate Interface Construction for Bounded-Context Long-Horizon Reasoning

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Addresses redundancy accumulation, context overflow, and error anchoring in long chain-of-thought reasoning under bounded context windows.
  • Argues the real bottleneck for long-horizon reasoning isn't trajectory compression, and instead proposes a learnable intermediate interface to reset reasoning state.

TAPR: Enhancing LLM Performance with a Task-Aware Prompt Rewriter

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Introduces a Task-Aware Prompt Rewriter (TAPR) model that automatically reformulates user prompts to unlock more of an LLM's capability.
  • Targeted at lowering the prompt-engineering skill barrier for non-expert users.

Chain-of-Models: Cross-Model Auditing for Bias-Robust LLM Judges

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Targets cognitive-bias vulnerability in LLM-as-judge setups, where existing mitigations rely on brittle prompt-driven debiasing or evaluation that doesn't scale.
  • Proposes cross-model auditing (chaining models to check each other's judgments) as a more bias-robust alternative.

Imbalanced Data Clustering via Targeted Data Augmentation Using GMM and LLM

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Addresses unsupervised clustering's tendency to miss underrepresented/minority topics in NLP datasets.
  • Combines Gaussian Mixture Models with targeted LLM-based data augmentation to improve coverage of minority topics.

  • Critiques the dominant approach to agent self-improvement (trajectory-level reflection) as operating only on episodic hindsight.
