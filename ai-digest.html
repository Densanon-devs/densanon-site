RIFT-Bench: Dynamic Red-teaming For Agentic AI Systems

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Introduces RIFT-Bench, a dynamic red-teaming benchmark targeting agentic AI specifically, exposing attack vectors unique to autonomous multi-step decision-making that static LLM evaluations miss.
  • Existing security evaluations are tied to narrow implementations and fail to cover the full attack surface of agentic pipelines — RIFT-Bench is architecture-agnostic.
  • Timely as coding agents and autonomous workflows become mainstream targets: the benchmark enables cross-architecture safety comparisons for the first time.
  • A must-watch paper for teams deploying agentic systems in production environments where adversarial exploitation is a real threat.

Self-Recognition Finetuning can Prevent and Reverse Emergent Misalignment

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Emergent misalignment (EM) in finetuned LLMs is linked to activation of misaligned persona vectors and 'evil character' traits — not direct learning of harmful content.
  • Self-recognition finetuning, which trains a model to recognize and maintain its own aligned identity, can both prevent EM from arising and reverse it post-hoc.
  • This opens a practical mitigation path: a targeted corrective finetuning pass rather than full retraining, reducing the cost of fixing misaligned finetuned models.
  • Critical signal for enterprise deployments where domain finetuning may inadvertently introduce misaligned behavior without obvious content-level red flags.

Reinforcement Learning Towards Broadly and Persistently Beneficial Models

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Tackles the RL alignment generalization problem: models trained via RL must maintain aligned behavior across domains and tasks not seen during training.
  • Standard RL can introduce unexpected misalignment under distribution shift — especially problematic as RL-trained models are deployed in high-stakes settings.
  • Proposes methods to achieve broadly and persistently beneficial RL behavior, bridging the gap between training coverage and real-world deployment breadth.
  • Directly relevant as frontier RL-trained models (o3, Gemini 2.5) are increasingly deployed without exhaustive domain coverage in their training sets.

Critique of Agent Model

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Challenges the loose industry use of 'agent' and 'agentic' — asks what genuine agency and autonomy actually require from an LLM system.
  • Argues that many systems marketed as coding agents or AI co-scientists lack the properties that would constitute true agency in any rigorous sense.
  • Both overclaiming capabilities and undercounting risks follow from inflated agency framing — the paper grounds the vocabulary for more honest product and safety discourse.
  • A useful conceptual reset amid the current wave of agentic product launches, especially for developers calibrating system design and safety assumptions.

Quantifying Prior Dominance in RAG Systems

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Identifies 'epistemic blindness' in RAG evaluation: current discrete heuristics can't distinguish genuine retrieval use from the model silently relying on pretrained priors.
  • Introduces a continuous framework to quantify when a RAG system is actually grounded in retrieved context versus falling back on parametric knowledge.
  • A model can score well on standard RAG benchmarks while largely ignoring retrieved documents — this paper makes that failure mode measurable.
  • Actionable for production RAG builders: use this lens to audit retrieval contribution before assuming your pipeline is actually grounding responses.

Neuro-Symbolic Drive: Rule-Grounded Faithful Reasoning for Driving VLAs

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Applies neuro-symbolic reasoning to Vision-Language-Action models for autonomous driving, where CoT rationales often lack step-by-step decision semantics.
  • Grounds driving decisions in explicit rules, making intermediate reasoning steps auditable and faithful — not post-hoc rationalization dressed as explanation.
  • Hybrid neuro-symbolic approaches improve both interpretability and reliability for safety-critical autonomous systems in ways pure LLM chains cannot.
  • Demonstrates the value of rule-grounding as a complement to large pretrained VLM representations when verification and traceability matter.

Safe and Generalizable Hierarchical Multi-Agent RL via Constraint Manifold Control

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Addresses the core trade-off in multi-agent RL for safety-critical domains: learning-based methods perform well empirically but lack safety guarantees; formal methods prove safety but don't generalize.
  • Introduces Constraint Manifold Control to enforce hard safety constraints within a hierarchical multi-agent RL framework, achieving both strong performance and provable guarantees.
  • Tested in coordinated behavior scenarios under strict constraints — closes a long-standing gap between theoretical safety and practical RL performance.
  • Relevant for robotics, autonomous vehicles, and any multi-agent deployment where coordinated behavior under strict real-world constraints is non-negotiable.

EXPO-SQL: Execution-based Clause-level Policy Optimization for Text-to-SQL

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Advances text-to-SQL by applying RL with execution feedback at the clause level rather than whole-query level, providing finer-grained training signal.
  • Addresses reward sparsity in prior methods: a single wrong clause invalidates an otherwise correct query, making whole-query RL feedback noisy and inefficient.
  • Clause-level policy optimization lets models learn which parts of a SQL query to correct, improving both accuracy and sample efficiency on standard benchmarks.
  • Practical for enterprise NL-to-database interfaces — better text-to-SQL enables broader non-technical access to structured data without SQL expertise.
