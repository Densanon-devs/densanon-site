Decision Context: Persistent Behavioral Learning for AI Agents

ArXiv cs.AI
  • forge_dc compiles human corrections and the agent's own failed-tool-call reviews into imperative RULE directives that carry across sessions.
  • Rules are injected at the decision points they govern, not retrieved as general context.
  • On the 71-scenario DC-Bench, compliance rose from 55% (naive retrieval) to 95%, with about 10x fewer injected tokens per query.

Forcing a Letter Suppresses Refusal: Mechanistic Evidence from WMDP

ArXiv cs.CL
  • On Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, multiple-choice prompts make the model commit to an answer letter very early, while open-ended prompts build toward refusal gradually.
  • MCQ activations sit about 3x further from the refusal direction than open-ended ones.
  • Ablating the heads that carry the answer letter brings refusal back, so MCQ safety scores mostly measure knowledge, not safety behaviour.

Aspect-wise Pairwise Tournaments for Test-Time Selection

ArXiv cs.AI
  • APT picks the best of n candidates with a single-elimination bracket, where each match is a vote among off-the-shelf verifiers, one per aspect.
  • It beats self-consistency, binary multi-aspect approval and reward-model selection across five domains, with the biggest gains on open-ended tasks.
  • Nothing is trained: aspects are plain-language prompts, so you can move it to a new domain or steer it without regenerating.

Your Experts Have Partners. There's HOPE for Keeping Them Together

Hugging Face Blog
  • HOPE prunes MoE experts by modelling which pairs of experts work together, solved as a quadratic program in seconds per layer.
  • Qwen3.5-35B pruned 50% scored 42.3 on SWE-Bench Pro against 41.8 unpruned; it ranked best on average across Qwen3.5-122B/35B and GLM-4.5-Air.
  • Its lead grows at higher pruning rates and on agentic coding. The code is open at github.com/awslabs/HOPE.
