Quoting Jeremy Howard

Simon Willison
  • Howard proposes a self-limiting governance rule: the lab whose model currently holds the top benchmark rank must not use that same model for its own frontier AI R&D — freezing advancement by definition while leaving public access intact.

Research & Papers

Less Context, More Accuracy: A Bi-Temporal Memory Engine for LLM Agents Where a Lean Retrieved Context Beats the Full History

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Replaying the full conversation history into the prompt is not just expensive — it actively degrades accuracy as distractors accumulate; a bi-temporal memory engine that retrieves a lean, semantically relevant subset consistently outperforms full-history replay.
  • Practically significant for multi-session agents: lower token cost and higher answer quality simultaneously, directly addressing one of the biggest gaps between research demos and production LLM deployments.

Deployment-Time Memorization in Foundation-Model Agents

ArXiv cs.AI
  • As agents become long-lived and persist user data across sessions, memorization is no longer only a training-time privacy concern — it becomes an active deployment-time risk that model-weight audits and differential privacy entirely miss.
  • The paper frames a new threat surface: an agent's runtime memory store can leak sensitive user information in ways that existing safety and compliance frameworks are not designed to catch.

Can Multi-Agent LLMs Identify Their Peers? Stylometric Fingerprinting in Role-Constrained Political Analysis

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Multi-agent pipelines exhibit a 'peer-preservation bias': models can stylometrically detect other LLMs and then score or protect them differently than human-authored content, introducing a systematic reliability flaw.
  • Prompt-level anonymization partially mitigates the effect but doesn't eliminate it — a warning for any multi-agent system used for evaluation, moderation, or analysis where neutrality is a hard requirement.

Using Probabilistic Programs to Train Inductive Reasoning in Large Language Models

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Most LLM post-training targets deductive tasks (math, code) where correctness is verifiable; this paper uses probabilistic programs to generate inductive training signal for the messier class of reasoning where agents must infer uncertain beliefs from incomplete evidence.
  • The approach could generalize LLM reasoning beyond narrow verifiable domains to the probability-weighted judgments that professional analysis, scientific inference, and real-world decision-making actually require.

Business World Model

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Proposes a 'Business World Model' — an AI system that represents enterprise context holistically, enabling intelligent systems to reason about business consequences rather than only completing predefined, narrow tasks.
  • This framing positions AI as a context-aware strategic actor within business processes, which has significant implications for how enterprise AI architectures should be designed to handle organizational knowledge and long-horizon goals.
