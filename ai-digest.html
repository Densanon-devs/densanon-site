What happened after 2,000 people tried to hack my AI assistant

Simon Willison
  • Fernando Irarrázaval ran a public prompt-injection challenge: 2,000 participants, 6,000 attempts, $500 in token spend — and the secret held.
  • Most attack vectors were standard jailbreak patterns; the assistant's system-prompt isolation and output filtering proved surprisingly robust.
  • Key finding: the cost of a successful attack, not invulnerability, is the real defense metric — budget-constrained attackers gave up, determined ones may not.
