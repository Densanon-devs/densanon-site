AI Morning Digest

Today's AI digest centers on agent behavior and safety: new papers on self-improving agents, multi-objective workflow generation, agent-to-agent manipulation, RAG abstention and forecasting misalignment from training data. Research & Papers

AI Agents are Vulnerable to Radicalization

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Researchers simulate conversations between two LLM agents, a target role-playing a human persona and another agent trying to influence it.
  • The study asks whether and how LLMs can manipulate each other's beliefs, a question little explored so far.

AREX-2: Advancing Self-Improving Agents through Long-Horizon Reflective Tasks

ArXiv cs.AI
  • AREX-2 targets self-improving LLM agents, defined as iteratively refining a solution at test time.
  • It rests on two complementary capabilities: reflection and solution refinement, evaluated on long-horizon reflective tasks.

MoFlow: Multi-Objective Agentic Workflow Generation

ArXiv cs.AI
  • MoFlow studies generating agentic workflows that jointly optimize accuracy, cost, latency, robustness and consistency.
  • The authors note existing methods typically optimize accuracy alone or a weighted sum of objectives.
Read full article

Sieve and Sage: Efficient Distraction Filtering for Reliable RALM Abstention

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Sieve and Sage addresses teaching retrieval-augmented LMs to abstain when retrieved evidence cannot support a reliable answer.
  • It argues against relying on monolithic LLMs for this and instead proposes efficient distraction filtering.

Alignment Forecasting: Predicting Misalignment From Training Data

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Examines how training on data with a narrow flaw can make a model broadly misaligned.
  • Proposes forecasting such misalignment from the training data, since today it is typically caught only after training by auditing the resulting model.
