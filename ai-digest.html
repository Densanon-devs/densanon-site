Research & Papers

Beyond Fixed Budgets: Characterizing the Inelasticity and Limitations of Tree-of-Thought Reasoning Strategies

ArXiv cs.AI
  • ToT reasoning strategies show high inelasticity — doubling the compute budget rarely doubles quality gain, and different search strategies plateau at very different ceilings.
  • Fixed-topology ToT is highly sensitive to role assignment and positional ordering of thoughts, meaning published benchmark numbers often don't transfer to production prompts.
  • Practitioners should benchmark their own task-specific ToT configurations rather than assuming published budget-quality curves apply.

PEAR: Permutation-Equivariant Adaptive Routing Multi-Agent Debate

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Fixed debate topologies in multi-agent LLM systems amplify positional bias and unreliable agents; PEAR introduces permutation-equivariant routing to dynamically reassign roles each round.
  • Adaptive routing reduces sensitivity to agent ordering and prevents a single low-quality model from consistently poisoning the consensus.
  • The approach is architecture-agnostic and can wrap existing multi-LLM debate pipelines without retraining any of the participating models.

Specifying AI-SDLC Processes: A Protocol Language for Human-Agent Boundaries

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Proposes a formal protocol language for expressing human-agent responsibility splits, approval gates, and governance constraints in AI-assisted software development.
  • Fills a real gap: AI agents act as first-class SDLC participants but no standard spec exists for encoding what they can do autonomously vs. what requires human sign-off.
  • Directly applicable to codegen pipelines — teams can encode 'agent writes, human approves merge' policies in a machine-verifiable format.

Post-Training Recipe, More Than Model Family, Shapes Multi-Agent LLM Conversational Behavior

ArXiv cs.CL
  • In multi-LLM debate and judge systems, the post-training alignment recipe (RLHF flavor, instruction-tuning style) matters more than base model family for behavioral diversity.
  • Models from the same family but different fine-tune runs converge toward similar outputs, reducing the value of model diversity in ensemble setups.
  • For genuine multi-agent behavioral diversity, you need different post-training pipelines — not just different model sizes or architectures.

Less is More: Lightweight Prompt Compression for Question Answering on Edge Devices

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Aggressive prompt compression for RAG on edge/constrained devices can match or beat uncompressed accuracy by stripping retrieval noise before it reaches the LLM.
  • Lightweight compression (token-budget aware, no auxiliary models needed) adds minimal latency while enabling larger effective context windows on small devices.
  • Key insight for local-LLM deployments: compress retrieved chunks before injecting rather than truncating generation, preserving more useful context per token.

Prompt Injection as Role Confusion

Simon Willison
  • Reframes prompt injection through a 'role confusion' lens: attacks succeed because models fail to maintain a stable model of who is trusted to issue which instructions.
  • The paper argues defenses should harden role boundaries rather than filter content, since content filtering is always bypassable with clever framing.
  • Directly actionable for agentic system designers: explicit role-gating in system prompts is more durable than input sanitization alone.
