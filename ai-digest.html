Alignment Faking Depends on the Value, Not Just the Model

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Tests 32 ValueBench dimensions across ten open- and closed-weight models (8B to about 675B parameters) under bare, unmonitored and monitored conditions.
  • Compliance gaps between conditions range from 0.05 to 0.50, and a distilled 70B model outperformed a roughly 675B model, so size is not the whole story.
  • Most variance comes from the specific model-value pairing; the authors propose 'value cards' that extend model cards with behavioral profiles.

MCPGuard: Finding Conformance Gaps in MCP SDKs via Dual-Track Analysis and Dynamic Adjudication

ArXiv cs.AI
  • An LLM-based framework compares the MCP specification with SDK code using a structural track (JSON Schema constraints) and a behavioral track (normative prose rules), then validates candidates with proof-of-concept tests.
  • It confirmed 13 findings in the Python SDK 1.26.0 and 19 in the TypeScript SDK 1.29.0; none of the 32 were exposed by the two black-box baselines.
  • Tests on open MCP servers suggest SDK gaps can propagate to downstream applications.

Why Pay for Long Reasoning When Rewrite Do Trick? Causal Effects of Reasoning History Compaction on Future Generation and Agent Cost

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Open-weight models rewrite their earlier reasoning into compact state records, without retraining, and continuations are compared against the original history.
  • Accepted compact histories usually shorten later reasoning, and in a coding case study they reduced repeated verification of a shared candidate solution.
  • In software-agent deployments compaction can cut total spending, but task quality and rewrite-inclusive time depend on the target model and compressor.

StateBench: Testing how language models track state changes over long documents

Hugging Face Blog
  • Ai2's benchmark has four difficulty levels grounded in Krohn-Rhodes theory, from set-once values to chained variable reassignment (the S5 permutation problem).
  • Frontier models handle the easy levels but fall to 13% or lower on the longest general finite problems; pretrained open models stay at chance on that level.
  • Gated DeltaNet and hybrid models trained from scratch (275M) generalized to sequences about 250 times longer than training, while transformers never learned the hardest level.

CWE-bench v2: More coverage, quality, and transparency

Hugging Face Blog
  • A benchmark of 100 tasks across 91 CWEs in which agents find and fix a security weakness in a real repository, with deterministic programmatic scoring and a held-out set.
  • Each task was hand-reviewed against six criteria, including that verifiers reject memorized upstream patches.
  • Of 11 models tested, the leader reaches 67.8% pass@1 and still fails 19% of tasks on every attempt; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash hits 57.0% pass@1 at $0.04 per rollout.

Palette: teaching a small model to build decks in your design language

Hugging Face Blog
  • IBM Research adapts a 20B open-weight model with LoRA to build slide decks in the IBM Design Language, using a designer role that writes a JSON brief and a coder role that writes pptxgenjs per slide.
  • Trained on about 1,000 synthetic decks; a geometry checker and editor model add 3.5 points on average across 131 decks, and parallel slide coding is about 6-7x faster.
  • On PresentBench its three-domain mean is 59.7, second place and 1.1 points behind NotebookLM.

Qwen 3.8 27B on my laptop can interpret model weights, too

Simon Willison
  • Responds to the 'Sparks of Weight-Space Programming' paper, which claims frontier models can read and edit raw network weights, for example fixing reasoning loops in Qwen3.5-0.8B by editing 0.016% of weights.
  • Willison tested the no-tools case: given weights of small policy nets trained on 10x10 games, local Qwen 3.8 27B identified Pac-Man, Pong and Breakout (taking 46 to 65 minutes each) but failed on Freeway.
  • He notes critics say the paper mixes reading weights with tool-assisted editing, and he is not ready to declare source code obsolete.
