Towards an Argumentative Foundation for Evaluative AI

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Proposes 'Evaluative AI' (EAI): instead of one recommendation, the system presents competing hypotheses with evidence for and against each, to support rather than replace human decision-making
  • Argues computational argumentation should be the formal, computable foundation for EAI because it can be made explainable and contestable
  • A position paper laying groundwork for a longer-term research agenda toward distributed, human-centered decision-support systems

Flow-by-Flow: Content-Judgment Bypass for Governing AI Output in High-Loss Domains

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Argues human oversight breaks down not from AI speed alone but from V x L — output velocity times per-item cognitive load (triage + judgment + response)
  • Only the 'judgment' component of that load shrinks as models improve; triage and response costs stay flat or get restructured rather than reduced, so oversight capacity doesn't scale with capability
  • Proposes 'Flow-by-Flow': govern via structural friction (a countable cognitive-cost score plus an institutional capacity cap) instead of judging output correctness directly
  • A Monte Carlo analysis across 1,000 parameter draws found multi-metric flow control beat simply adding more human supervision in 90.8% of trials

Training Variable Long Sequences with Data-Centric Parallel

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Introduces Data-Centric Parallel (DCP): each batch's sequence length dynamically drives runtime settings (parallel size, gradient accumulation, recomputation) instead of using static configs
  • Delivers up to 2.88x speedup on 32 H200 GPUs versus existing baseline approaches
  • Drop-in design — integrates into any model with about 10 lines of code, positioned as a reusable baseline for distributed training on variable-length sequences

Unified Hallucination Fuzzing for Multimodal Large Language Models

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Introduces UniHall, a fine-grained hallucination benchmark spanning Object, Instruction, and Knowledge dimensions, plus SAMF (Self-Adaptive Multimodal Fuzzing), which evolves adversarial test cases instead of relying on static, saturating benchmarks
  • State-of-the-art MLLMs show significant performance degradation once fuzzed versus standard benchmark settings, exposing a gap between reasoning ability and factual grounding
  • Finds a 'helpfulness-hallucination trade-off': RL alignment tuning inadvertently increases sycophancy in instruction-following, worsening hallucination rates

DocAtlas: Long-Document Understanding as Mutable-State Interaction

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Treats long-document QA as a mutable-state process — a document harness controls what's searched, read, stored, and shown to the model at each step, rather than using a static retrieval index
  • With GPT-5.4, hits 71.4% accuracy on MMLongBench-Doc, beating the 65.8% human-expert reference score
  • A 4B-parameter Qwen VLM trained end-to-end with RL inside the DocAtlas environment reaches 63.7% accuracy, a 9.3-point jump over feeding the same model the raw document directly (54.4%)

Search-G1: Grounded Search Agents via Representation-Based Intrinsic Rewards

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Trains search agents to retrieve only when necessary and ground answers in evidence, using two internal reward 'readouts' instead of costly human or LLM-judge annotations — one estimates closed-book sufficiency, the other measures how much the answer depends on retrieved evidence
  • Reward signals are periodically refit as the policy improves during training, avoiding reward staleness
  • Across multiple QA benchmarks, produces shorter response trajectories (lower search cost) while holding accuracy steady — a better grounding-versus-cost tradeoff than prior reward approaches

Scaling Inherently Interpretable Language Models

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Challenges the assumption that interpretability taxes capability by building interpretability into the training objective itself rather than explaining models after the fact
  • Tested across three orders of magnitude of compute on both autoregressive and diffusion models — interpretability scales up with capability, with representations becoming more human-concept-aligned as scale grows
  • Ships Steerling-8B, a diffusion LM with causal attention that attributes any generated output to specific input tokens, concepts, and training data, enabling diagnose-and-steer fixes without retraining
  • Steerling-8B stays competitive with peer models trained on 2-16x more compute

Build Low-Latency Multilingual Voice Agents: Open Weights & Full Deployment Control with NVIDIA Magpie TTS

Hugging Face Blog
  • 364M-parameter open-weights multilingual TTS model covering 12 languages, including newly added Arabic, Korean, and Brazilian Portuguese
  • 32ms time-to-first-audio on an NVIDIA B200, with 320x real-time throughput at 64 concurrent streams
