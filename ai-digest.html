Research & Papers

SDAD: Spec-Driven Agentic Development for the AI-Native SDLC

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Proposes restructuring the software development lifecycle around frontier coding agents that now carry context windows from hundreds of thousands up to millions of tokens.
  • Frames agentic coding around explicit, checkable specs rather than ad hoc prompting, aiming to make multi-step agent-driven development more reliable and auditable.
  • Relevant to anyone running Claude Code-style agents at scale — a formalized spec-driven workflow targets exactly the kind of drift that shows up over long agent sessions.

PrimeAgentOrchestrator: Memory-Primed Agent Spawning for Personal AI Infrastructure

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Tackles the cold-start problem where every new Claude Code agent session begins with an empty context window and discards prior accumulated knowledge.
  • PrimeAgentOrchestrator (PAO) spawns new Claude Code instances pre-loaded (“memory-primed”) with relevant prior history, aimed at persistent personal AI infrastructure rather than one-off tasks.
  • Directly on-topic for anyone running persistent multi-agent setups with hand-maintained memory files — this targets the exact context-continuity gap those files are currently patching manually.

Truth Lies Deep: Countering Semantic Camouflage via Latent Intent Verification

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Argues current LLM safety alignment is often superficial: refusal mechanisms trigger only at the final stages of generation without erasing the harmful knowledge acquired during pretraining.
  • Proposes verifying latent intent rather than relying solely on surface-level refusal triggers, targeting attacks that semantically camouflage harmful intent.
  • Relevant to anyone building or auditing guardrails for local/agentic LLM deployments — a concrete reminder that refusal-only alignment is bypassable by design.

Beyond Raw Transcripts: Structured Persona Extraction for LLM-Based Digital Twins

ArXiv cs.CL

  • Moves away from dumping raw transcripts into context, instead extracting a structured persona representation to ground the simulation.

ArXiv cs.CL

  • Evaluates specific points where these systems' vocabulary comprehension breaks down in ways that undermine actual clinical/safety reasoning.

Who Do Language Models Think Is Competent? A Mechanistic Analysis of Occupational Bias

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Investigates whether LLMs that pass behavioral bias evaluations have actually unlearned biased occupational associations, or just learned not to express them.
  • Uses mechanistic interpretability to probe internal representations directly, rather than trusting surface-level output behavior.
  • Matters for anyone treating “passes the bias eval” as a safety signal — the underlying associations may still be present, just suppressed at output time.

Inhibitory Attention for Clinical Long-Context Reasoning: Characterizing and Mitigating Lost-in-the-Middle Effects in EHR Processing

ArXiv cs.CL
  • Addresses the well-known “lost-in-the-middle” effect — where models retrieve information less reliably from the center of long contexts — specifically for electronic health records exceeding 100,000 tokens.
  • Proposes an inhibitory-attention mechanism to characterize and mitigate the effect rather than just documenting it.
  • Directly applicable to any long-context RAG or retrieval pipeline wrestling with the same middle-of-context degradation, not just clinical ones.
