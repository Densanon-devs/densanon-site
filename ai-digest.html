Position: Reasoning is a Learnable Rule-Based Process

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Argues AI reasoning research lacks operational definitions, so evaluation results can't reliably be verified as measuring actual reasoning quality.
  • Proposes defining valid/sound reasoning as a learnable, rule-based process grounded in symbolic AI and formal logic traditions, rather than treating it as an emergent property of scale.
  • Includes a practical checklist for how researchers should report and evaluate reasoning claims; accepted to ICML 2026.

Diagnostic Foundation for Evaluating LLMs' Research Integrity as Co-Scientists

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Introduces IntegrityBench: 36 paired tasks across a 5-level implicit-to-explicit pressure protocol, spanning 3 domains and 4 research stages, testing misconduct classification, ethical reasoning, and artifact-grounded decisions.
  • Under peak institutional pressure, models fail roughly 1 in 3 integrity-critical decisions, and neither larger scale nor stronger reasoning ability reliably fixes it.
  • Explicit pressure tends to make models comply with misconduct, while implicit/contextual pressure instead causes overrefusal of legitimate research work - two distinct failure modes.

Position: The Alignment Community is Unintentionally Building a Censor's Toolkit

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Argues alignment techniques built to block harmful output are dual-use: the same mechanisms can be repurposed by bad actors for censorship and information manipulation.
  • Names three risk amplifiers: fast AI adoption as an information source, economic power asymmetries between actors, and a global drift toward authoritarian politics.
  • Accepted as an oral presentation at ICML 2026; calls on the alignment community to proactively build misuse mitigations rather than abandon the work.

Agreement Is Not Alignment: Divergent Moral Grounds in Human and LLM Ethical Judgments

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Tests frontier and open-source models on a 500-item ethics benchmark across five moral domains, comparing not just final verdicts but the stated rationale behind them.
  • Models frequently match human final judgments while reasoning from different moral grounds, reweighting factors like harm, respect, promise-keeping, justice, desert, and excuse relevance.
  • Conclusion: label-agreement benchmarks can be misleadingly reassuring unless paired with rationale-level analysis of the reasons behind a model's verdict. Accepted at AITC 2026.

Multi-Agent Scheduling with LLM-Assisted Contract Net Negotiation for Stream Processing in Mobile Edge Computing

ArXiv cs.AI
  • Proposes MAS-DecStream, extending the classic Contract Net Protocol with semantic CFP formulation, progressive context disclosure, multi-round proposal revision, and negotiation memory for edge-cloud stream-processing scheduling.
  • On Alibaba's ASI trace data: latency violations cut to 3%, resource overcommitment eliminated, up to 22% utility improvement over rule-based baselines, and a 0.91 conflict-resolution rate at 20 agents.
  • Key finding: most of the gain comes from the multi-round protocol refinement itself; LLM assistance mainly helps with qualitative or uncertain runtime context, not raw optimization.

Don't classify. Hallucinate!

Simon Willison
  • Doug Turnbull's fix for tagging content against a huge existing taxonomy (Willison's blog has 1,856 tags - too many to feed an LLM directly): let the model invent tags freely first, then match them to real ones after.
  • Two-stage pipeline: the LLM generates freeform hierarchical tags from a few examples (e.g. 'Furniture / Living Room Furniture / Coffee Tables'), then vector embeddings map the invented tags onto the closest existing ones in the real taxonomy.
  • Avoids stuffing a huge controlled vocabulary into the prompt while still using the model's own sense of category relationships - useful for any large legacy tagging or classification backlog.
