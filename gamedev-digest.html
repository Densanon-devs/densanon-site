Three Epic veterans are building an AI-powered game engine to break the industry's doom cycle

GamesIndustry.biz
  • The engine, called Immens, comes from three Unreal/Epic veterans: Arjan Brussee (ex-UE5 Technical Director who led Fortnite's port to mobile, Switch, and next-gen consoles), Michal Valient (led the Decima Engine tech behind Horizon Zero Dawn and Killzone at Guerrilla), and Sjoerd 'Hourences' De Jong (27 years on Unreal, most recently Senior Director for Developer Experience at Epic).

  • The founders frame deep AI integration in the toolchain as a way to break the industry's crunch-and-layoffs 'doom cycle,' not just a feature bullet point.

Memory crisis is driving uncertainty over PlayStation 6's launch date

Game Developer
  • Sony CEO Hiroki Totoki told the Wall Street Journal the company still has no fixed PS6 launch date, directly citing supply constraints.
  • The root cause is a global memory-chip (DRAM) shortage driven by AI data center buildouts pulling production away from consumer hardware -- Microsoft, Nintendo, and Valve have all adjusted pricing because of the same crunch.
  • Sony says it has secured enough memory to hit FY26 sales targets for existing PS5 stock, but the shortage is the specific reason next-gen timing keeps slipping rather than a design or manufacturing issue.

Game Developer

Bain & Co: Research shows only one in five players seek out new titles

GamesIndustry.biz
  • Bain's 2026 Gaming Report (survey of 5,300+ gamers plus analysis of 100 titles released since 2023) found two-thirds of players default to sequels or familiar genres, and only one in five actively seek out something new.
  • Games built around a clearly identifiable target player achieved commercial success 83% of the time, versus just 50% for games without a defined player profile -- a concrete data point favoring narrow positioning over broad appeal.
  • Global gaming software revenue is growing at roughly a 3% compound annual rate and is projected to hold that pace for four more years, underscoring how hard discoverability is getting for original titles in a slow-growth market.

Arco tops 100,000 sales in two years and breaks even

Game Developer
  • Arco, a Panic-published Mesoamerican-fantasy RPG with simultaneous turn-based combat, passed 100,000 copies sold and only became profitable two years after its August 2024 launch.
  • Sales came primarily through Steam (with notable Steam Deck uptake), alongside Switch, Mac App Store, and Epic Games Store releases.
  • Composer/sound designer Jose Ramon: 'It's incredible to believe that more than 100,000 people have played -- and hopefully enjoyed -- something we worked so hard on,' a reminder that even a well-reviewed indie RPG can take years to turn a profit.

WINGS really, really wants to fund your neat indie video game

Game Developer
  • WINGS, an investor focused on women and gender-marginalized developers, launched three funding pathways: white-label publishing (go-to-market, ads, content, and biz-dev support), self-publishing partnerships (WINGS covers dev and marketing costs while the studio executes), and finishing funds (cash injections for near-release projects, best fit for budgets under $100k with a 3-6 month runway to launch).
  • Ideal project budgets are $500,000 or less including marketing; larger budgets require a confirmed financing plan already in place.
  • There are no application rounds -- pitches are accepted continuously through an online form, and developers retain full creative and business control regardless of which funding type they get.

How Forbidden Solitaire brought solitaire games to the forefront

Game Developer
  • Grey Alien Games (Jake Birkett, seven prior solitaire titles) teamed with Night Signal Entertainment (Home Safety Hotline) to pair solitaire mechanics with a 90s-era analog-horror aesthetic.
  • The game sold 10,000 copies in its first 48 hours (April 2026), peaked at 880 concurrent players, and picked up 7,000 overnight Steam wishlists after its teaser trailer went viral -- dwarfing the studio's earlier solitaire games (Regency Solitaire II peaked at 98 concurrent, Shadowhand Solitaire at 20).
  • A deliberate design choice drove the jump: tri-peaks/golf solitaire rules instead of Klondike, chosen specifically for faster, arcade-y, combo-building play that matched the horror theme's audience better than a traditional ruleset.
