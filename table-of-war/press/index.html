Description

A tactics roguelike deckbuilder where you command an army, not a hero. Units move and fight on their own action points; your deck calls in reinforcements, ordnance and orders. Every soldier has a name, a rank, and exactly one life. Build the army. Read the board. Lose them anyway.

Most deckbuilders hand you one fighter and a stack of verbs. Table of War hands you a company. Your units walk, flank and hold ground on their own, no card required. The deck sits on top as your command channel: reinforcements, ordnance, standing orders, structures, and the fuel for your general's signature power. The board is the tactics. The deck is the strategy.

Your roster is persistent and permanent. Troops carry their name, rank, experience and scars from battle to battle, and they do not come back. Battles play out on procedurally generated, non-rectangular maps with fog of war, zones of control, flanking, terrain, drifting hazards and artillery that does not check whose uniform it lands on. Enemy intent is telegraphed on your turn, so every loss is a decision you made.

Three commanders play three different games: the Warlord's cheap reinforcements and Wrath, the Engineer's structures and Overcharge, and the Nomad, whose HQ is a vehicle.

At a glance

  • 3 commanders, a 4-act branching expedition, a permadeath roster
  • 176 cards, 38 relics, 56 events, 9 bosses, 57 achievements, 12 ascension rungs, 7 run modifiers
  • Procedural non-rectangular battlefields, fog of war, zones of control, hazards, friendly fire
  • Seeded runs and a Daily Commission everyone plays on the same seed
