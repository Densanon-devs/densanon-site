Robotics Morning Digest

Research this cycle clusters around making autonomous systems safer under uncertainty: a failure-aware world-action model that learns from bad actions instead of discarding them, a Unitree G1 humanoid planner for whole-body movement through confined spaces, a constrained-RL approach to human-following in crowds, and a statistical framework showing how coarse operational data can make AV safety claims dangerously optimistic. On the industry side, capital keeps flowing into autonomy at scale — Cambridge Aerospace raised a $300M Series C for its drone interceptors, and DAF/Einride and PlusAI both pushed their autonomous-trucking timelines toward 2027 commercial launch — while a KIT-built disassembly robot points at a more circular approach to electronics recycling. On the tooling side, the ROS community is leaning on LLMs and better dependency tooling to make debugging and workspace management less painful.

Research & Papers

FACT: Failure-Aware Causal Training for World-Action Models

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Most world-action models train almost exclusively on successful demonstrations and have no signal for what happens after a bad action; FACT instead conditions predictions on the executed action and treats failures as valid future targets rather than discarding them.
  • Predicts both future video frames and task progress, using failure rollouts as a supervisory signal; at inference the progress predictor can score candidate actions using both success and failure outcomes.
  • Outperforms baselines on simulated and real-world bimanual manipulation, improves as more failure data is added, and reduces the 'success-biased' hallucination where the model predicts a good outcome even from a bad action.

Whole-Body Planning for Humanoids Navigating Confined Spaces via Self-Collision Avoidance References

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Targets humanoid locomotion in tight spaces where dense obstacles and self-collision bounds break standard trajectory optimizers; validated on a Unitree G1.
  • Three-stage pipeline: kinematic path planning over reachable rigid-body volumes, differentiable collision avoidance folded into reachability-constrained optimization, then residual RL trained on the optimized trajectories for real-world robustness.
  • Handles confinement ratios below 1.5 and 12-18 second multi-contact tasks (multiple foot/hand contacts) where standard baseline optimizers simply fail to find a feasible trajectory.

Navigating the Proximity-Safety Balance: Constraint Decomposition for Human Following in Pedestrian Crowds

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Reframes 'follow a person through a crowd' as a multi-constraint RL problem instead of one dense reward, splitting it into a sparse following reward plus separate, interpretable cost constraints for safety margins.
  • Feeds uncertainty estimates about pedestrian motion prediction directly into the RL cost terms, rather than relying on a fixed safety buffer, so the robot can adapt closeness to how predictable nearby pedestrians are.
  • Validated on a real robot deployment and shown to hold up in out-of-distribution crowd scenarios; accepted to IROS 2026.

The Impact of Operational-Data Fidelity when Assessing Safety-Critical Autonomous-Vehicle Software

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Shows that reliability claims for safety-critical AV software can be 'dangerously optimistic' when the operational data behind them isn't granular enough, even when the assessor tries to be conservative.
  • Extends conservative Bayesian inference (CBI) to explicitly test how much data coarseness erodes the validity of a reliability claim, rather than assuming conservative math alone protects against bad data.
  • Delivers what the authors call the first conservative estimates of how data fidelity affects these assessments — relevant to anyone certifying AV software off field logs rather than fine-grained event data.

Robot Recycler Salvages Parts From Broken Machines

IEEE Spectrum Robotics
  • KIT (Karlsruhe Institute of Technology) researchers built a disassembly robot that combines CAD/mathematical defect prediction with robotic manipulation, testing each part's actual degrees-of-freedom movement to diagnose what's broken rather than assuming from spec.
  • Adapts its strategy live: if a screw is stuck, it switches to milling or another method instead of persisting, while still trying to preserve valuable parts undamaged.
  • Long-term vision from the team is a '100-armed' disassembly factory line aimed at making repair cheaper than manufacturing new units; presented at ICRA 2026 in Vienna.

Analyzing rosbags with LLMs and SQL + queryable Nav2 behavior trees

ROS Discourse
  • Compares two ROS-community approaches to LLM-assisted rosbag analysis: mcap-mcp-server, which loads rosbag data into a queryable database so LLMs can just write SQL instead of needing bespoke analysis tools, and Pixel Robotics' bt-rosbag-analysis agent skill.
  • The database-first approach covers standard rosbag data (trajectories, laser scans, tf) as well as extended Nav2 behavior-tree logs, all through the same SQL interface.
  • Signals a broader shift in ROS tooling toward letting the LLM write SQL against structured logs rather than building dedicated natural-language-to-robotics-analysis tools.

rostree v1.0.0: explore your ROS 2 dependency graph from the terminal, or in your browser

ROS Discourse
  • Fixes a structural bug in earlier versions: a ROS dependency graph is a DAG, not a tree, but 0.2.2 expanded every path separately, producing tens of thousands of duplicate nodes; v1.0 expands each package once at first appearance.
  • Performance impact is dramatic — unlimited-depth traversal drops from 58,002 lines/10.29s to 251 lines/0.21s, roughly 50x faster with orders of magnitude less output.
  • Ships as a CLI, an interactive TUI, and a Python API, with package discovery done once into an index and memoized package.xml parsing.
