AI can now control fusion plasma faster than humans can react

ScienceDaily Robotics
  • Princeton/PPPL's "PACMAN" system combines reinforcement learning with predictive plasma-behavior models, running full control loops in about 20 milliseconds versus seconds for a human operator.
  • Tested on the DIII-D tokamak, it predicted a tearing-mode instability roughly 200ms before onset and adjusted plasma conditions to prevent it from forming at all, something conventional controllers can't do.
  • The system coordinated all six of DIII-D's gyrotrons simultaneously, optimizing heating power and mirror aim in real time; results published in Nuclear Fusion (2026).

Cyborg Roaches Can Stab You With Needles

IEEE Spectrum Robotics
  • "Paraborg" cockroaches steer via electrodes on their antennae/cerci controlled with a handheld game controller, and carry a spring-loaded injector that uses a CO2-generating chemical reaction to fire a drug-filled syringe into a target.
  • Swarm design splits duties between reconnaissance (camera-carrying roaches) and medical intervention (injector-carrying roaches), aimed at reaching disaster victims trapped in rubble that conventional robots or rescuers can't access.
  • In proof-of-concept trials over a 2.5-meter obstacle course, the cyborgs completed all 25 attempts and hit their injection target with a 72% success rate.

Pressure sensors can help improve robotic gripping accuracy

The Robot Report
  • Distributed tactile sensors (piezoresistive films, capacitive layers, microfluidic pads) measure localized stress at the contact interface rather than one global force reading, capturing the full grasp sequence: contact, surface deformation, micro-slips, load redistribution, then stable hold or failure.
  • Sensors can flag slip within the first few milliseconds of contact via characteristic micro-variations in pressure distribution, letting a controller react before an object actually drops.
  • Rigid, soft, and fragile objects load the sensor array differently (concentrated vs. broadly distributed vs. asymmetric collapse); demonstrated in the field with XELA Robotics' uSkin fingertip sensors on a UMI gripper.

ARM Institute gets $90M for 10 projects to modernize military manufacturing

The Robot Report
  • The $90M comes from the Office of the Undersecretary of War's Manufacturing Technology Office via an Organic Industrial Base Modernization Challenge, spread across 15 ARM Institute member organizations.
  • The 10 funded projects fall into three buckets: AI for safety and quality in energetics manufacturing, drones to speed mission readiness, and robotics to clear manufacturing bottlenecks, targeting 12 military manufacturing sites nationwide.
  • Two-year delivery window, with each project required to include a workforce-upskilling component alongside the technology itself.

Enovis to acquire surgical tech provider eCential Robotics for $180M

The Robot Report
  • Deal terms: €176M ($204.3M) at closing plus up to €35M ($40.6M) in milestone-based earnouts; eCential brings a 7-degree-of-freedom robotic arm and 50+ engineers built on 15+ years of computer-assisted surgery work.
  • Enovis plans to ship its first robotic system for knee surgery within two years, integrating eCential's proprietary control software into its existing ASTRA platform, before following with a shoulder system where Enovis already holds market share.
  • Transaction expected to close by end of 2026.
