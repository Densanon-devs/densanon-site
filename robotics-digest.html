Adversarial Stress Testing of SPARK Humanoid Safety Filters

ArXiv cs.RO
  • SPARK humanoid safety filters modify nominal control actions when collision-avoidance constraints may be violated — adversarial stress testing reveals failure modes at the boundary of those filters.
  • Humanoid robots present a uniquely hard safety problem: high-dimensional bodies, dense collision constraints, and operation near people mean standard filter margins may be insufficient under adversarial inputs.
  • The adversarial test methodology generates worst-case perturbations to nominal motion plans, producing a structured benchmark for comparing safety filter robustness across humanoid platforms.
  • Findings identify where filter conservatism must be raised vs. where it can be relaxed — directly applicable to industrial humanoid deployment criteria.

Distributionally Robust Control via Stein Variational Inference for Contact-Rich Manipulation

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Contact-rich manipulation introduces sharp uncertainty discontinuities that standard Gaussian models poorly represent — Stein Variational Inference maintains a richer, particle-based uncertainty estimate updated online per interaction.
  • The distributionally robust formulation optimizes control against the worst-case distribution within an uncertainty set, providing formal guarantees that degrade gracefully when contact dynamics shift at runtime.
  • Unlike large-scale data-driven methods that amortize uncertainty through dataset size, this approach adapts to rare or novel contact geometries without retraining.
  • Benchmarks show improved success rates under model mismatch compared to standard robust MPC baselines on contact-rich manipulation tasks.

KG-ASG: Collision-Knowledge-Guided Closed-Loop Adversarial Scenario Generation

ArXiv cs.RO
  • KG-ASG uses a collision knowledge graph to guide adversarial scenario generation toward semantically meaningful near-miss and collision events, addressing the gap between coverage and interpretability in AV safety validation.
  • The closed-loop generation loop ensures scenarios remain executable by the system under test, avoiding the common open-loop pitfall where adversarial trajectories become physically infeasible.
  • Primary-support attribution labels the causal agent vs. reactive participant in each collision, enabling targeted fault analysis rather than aggregate pass/fail metrics.
  • This structured approach generates high-risk scenarios that are both coverage-rich and semantically interpretable — a combination existing methods have not achieved simultaneously.

RLFTSim: Realistic and Controllable Multi-Agent Traffic Simulation via Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Supervised open-loop training for traffic simulation fails to capture emergent multi-agent interaction dynamics — RLFTSim fine-tunes pretrained simulation models with RL to close the behavioral distribution gap.
  • The RL objective rewards realistic multi-agent interaction fidelity and scenario controllability, allowing operators to dial in target traffic density and conflict frequency.
  • Closed-loop RL fine-tuning avoids compounding errors that accumulate when open-loop models are rolled out for extended simulation horizons.
  • RLFTSim generates diverse, controllable traffic scenarios for AV testing without requiring additional real-world data collection.

Fraunhofer IPA offers new test benchmark for humanoids

The Robot Report
  • Fraunhofer IPA developed a third-party benchmark for industrial humanoid evaluation, filling the gap left by vendor-run demos that lack standardized, application-relevant criteria.
  • The benchmark covers criteria directly tied to industrial deployment: payload, cycle time, dexterity under load, and safety proximity performance — not general locomotion or stair-climbing metrics.
  • Third-party independent testing is critical for buyers: without it, humanoid procurement decisions rely entirely on vendor-curated footage, which rarely shows failure modes.
  • IPA is making the benchmark available for external analysis, positioning it as a potential industry-standard evaluation framework as humanoid deployments scale past pilot programs.

Robots Could Turn E-Waste Into a Source of Legacy Chips

IEEE Spectrum Robotics
  • Robotic disassembly of e-waste is emerging as a practical path to recovering legacy chips — components no longer in production but still in high demand for industrial and defense applications.
  • New European waste-shipment rules, California's non-removable battery recycling fees, and Malaysia's e-waste import ban are creating simultaneous regulatory pressure that makes automated recovery economically viable.
  • Robotic disassembly must handle extreme product variety with no standardized fastening, driving demand for dexterous manipulation systems capable of operating on unpredictable, mixed-density assemblies.
  • Recovering functioning legacy ICs from e-waste boards could partially offset semiconductor supply shortages for sectors that cannot migrate to newer parts.
