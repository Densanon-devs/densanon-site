Research & Papers

Adaptation of Generalist Robot Policies with Minimal Data

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Introduces MiDAS, an offline-to-online RL recipe that anchors a pretrained VLA to a new task via behavior cloning on just one or a few demonstrations, then improves it with value-based online RL on a residual policy.
  • On the LIBERO and RoboCasa benchmarks, MiDAS recovers strong task performance from as little as one demonstration, outperforming baselines and generalizing beyond the demonstrated conditions.
  • On a real bimanual YAM robot, a fragile single-demo policy improved its robustness and learned new successful behaviors after roughly 6 hours of autonomous online interaction.
  • Authors describe it as the first reliable demonstration of robot policy adaptation from a single task demonstration.

Top-down Traffic Scenario Generation via Joint Initial-Goal Diffusion and Trajectory Infilling

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Proposes TrafficDiffuser, which jointly generates initial and goal state pairs for traffic agents via diffusion, instead of treating agent placement and trajectory generation as separate steps.
  • Making goal states explicit gives initial agent positions an interpretable reason for being there and reduces trajectory generation to a simpler infilling problem.
  • Evaluated on the Argoverse 2 motion prediction dataset; the generated high-level scenarios can constrain trajectory generation to specific driving modes and plug into existing trajectory generators.
  • Cuts speed-distribution distance by 55.3% and off-road rate by 2.8% versus the next-best agent-initialization approach.

From Self-Normal-Positioning to Omni-Directional Tracking: Real-Time Surface Modeling Enabled Probe Tilt Control for Robotic Ultrasound Imaging

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Most robotic ultrasound systems only hold the probe perpendicular to the skin, which fails for exams like echocardiography that need oblique probe angles; this framework instead tracks arbitrary orientations.
  • Fuses multi-view RGB-D point clouds into a real-time quadratic local-surface model, then encodes the desired imaging direction relative to that surface normal so the probe can follow non-normal angles.
  • Achieved mean angular tracking error of 1.06 ± 0.66 degrees and recovered non-normal tilts up to 44.39 ± 2.59 degrees, acquiring the target heart-chamber view in both phantom and in-vivo tests.

Herding End-to-End Autonomous Driving via Neuro-Symbolic Safety Guards

ArXiv cs.RO
  • End-to-end driving agents learn statistical patterns rather than the physical conditions that guarantee safety, so they can still break basic traffic rules despite high average scores; this paper adds a lightweight rule-based guard at the final command interface instead of retraining the model.
  • The guard checks each outgoing driving command against explicit safety rules and swaps in the nearest safe alternative only when needed — no learned component, no retraining, and every intervention is traceable to the rule that triggered it.
  • Applied to TransFuser v6 on the Fail2Drive and Bench2Drive long-tail benchmarks: +15% Success Rate and up to 53% fewer safety-critical collisions, while preserving the original Driving Score.

Robot orders increase to $622 million in Q2 as automation demand broadens across industries

Robotics & Automation News
  • North American companies ordered 8,940 robots worth $622M in Q2 2026, per new A3 data — up 4.3% in units and 21.3% in revenue year-over-year; H1 2026 totals reached 17,995 units / $1.166B.
  • Growth is broad outside automotive: Semi & Electronics/Photonics orders rose 35% in H1 (38% YoY in Q2 alone), Life Sciences/Pharma/Biomed rose 32%, and Automotive Component rose 24% — while Automotive OEM orders fell 25%.
  • Collaborative robots: 2,774 cobots worth $114M were ordered in H1 (15.4% of units), making up 43.7% of Life Sciences orders and 36.5% of Semi/Electronics orders.
  • A3 EVP Alex Shikany: "The breadth of growth outside Automotive OEM is an important trend we'll continue to watch."

Honda’s new ‘Avatar’ humanoid robot suggests the spirit of ASIMO is very much alive

Robotics & Automation News
  • Avatar is a teleoperated system, not an autonomous humanoid — the human operator keeps the decision-making loop while AI assists physical execution; it was demoed hands-on at Humanoids Summit 2026.
  • The hand alone: 16 actuated joints (up to 180°/s), 50N continuous fingertip force, 288 tactile-sensor channels across fingers/palm, and six-axis force sensors at the fingertips, tested through 450,000+ durability cycles including 24,000 cycles lifting 5kg loads.
  • Demonstrated dexterity spans threading a needle and using scissors up to tightening screws and handling heavy objects.
  • Honda never abandoned humanoid R&D after ASIMO left public view — engineers from that program carried the expertise into Avatar, but Honda split "fully autonomous humanoid" into separate teleoperation-plus-AI-assist technologies instead.

Strengthening U.S. Army sustainment: TALUS to deliver autonomous distribution

The Robot Report
  • Stratom Inc. (a Boulder, CO Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business) leads a team — ND Defense, Forterra, GS Engineering, Waltonen Engineering — on a $15.9M U.S. Army Project Sustainment contract for an 18-month prototype.
  • TALUS is a modular autonomous distribution system meant to resupply dispersed forces in contested environments, reducing soldier exposure while extending operational reach; it integrates militarized commercial-off-the-shelf tech and Forterra's AutoDrive autonomy stack.
  • Modular architecture supports multiple logistics/energy missions and varied payloads; the prototype phase ends with a soldier-run operational evaluation during a Transformation in Contact Combat Training Center rotation that will inform broader fielding decisions.

Robots could consume as much electricity as 35 million US homes by 2035

Robotics & Automation News
  • Wood Mackenzie projects combined robotics electricity demand hits 363 TWh/year by 2035 (357 TWh industrial, 6 TWh humanoid) — roughly equal to France's entire 2025 nuclear output (373 TWh) and about 35 million average US households.
  • Industrial robot fleet grows from 5M units today (78 TWh/yr) to a projected 16M units by 2035 at a 12% CAGR; humanoid stock grows at over 90% CAGR, exceeding 10M units with 4M+ annual shipments by 2035.
  • Estimates use a $0.14/kWh global average industrial tariff and deliberately exclude robot AI-training infrastructure, to avoid double-counting against data-center demand.
  • The report frames power availability itself — not just chips or manufacturing capacity — as an emerging brake on robot adoption, compounding existing grid pressure from AI data centers.

SLAM Camera Board + Obstacle Mapping

r/robotics
  • Mighty Camera runs visual-inertial odometry (VIO) on-device in real time in a tiny package, giving accurate camera-motion tracking without external infrastructure.
  • Combines that motion estimate with the live camera feed to estimate depth and build a 3D obstacle map, computed on the board/SDK itself.
