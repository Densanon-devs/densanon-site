Research & Papers

VLCP: Vision Language Control Policy Closed-Loop Code Replanning for Robot Manipulation

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Keeps the vision-language model frozen and has it write a short Python control function directly, instead of fine-tuning it to emit an unfamiliar action representation -- no demonstrations, no fine-tuning.
  • Closed-loop replanning: every K steps the VLM re-observes multi-view RGB and proprioceptive state and rewrites the control function, catching failures before they compound.
  • Hits 35.1% pooled success across 57 MuJoCo/RoboVerse tasks vs. 3.5% for a single-query open-loop variant -- a 10x gap -- with a 27.3% within-episode recovery rate on failed grasps.

FetchMan: Learning Visual Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Policies from Simulated Experiences

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Trains visual humanoid loco-manipulation policies entirely in simulation, across more than 150,000 simulated scenes, to sidestep costly real-world demonstration collection.
  • Combines behavioral cloning with Flow-GRPO reinforcement-learning refinement on sparse rewards to break through the performance ceiling that pure imitation learning hits.
  • Zero-shot transfer to a real Unitree G1 humanoid reaches 73.3% success on reach-and-pick across unseen scenes; the team also releases FetchMan-Bench as a new evaluation benchmark.

Lambda-Hold Control: Human-Like Movement Emerges from a Minimal Task Reward in Predictive Musculoskeletal Simulation

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Proposes a lambda-hold controller, inspired by the equilibrium-point hypothesis of human motor control, where the policy sets per-muscle equilibrium-length thresholds instead of directly commanding muscle excitations.
  • Sidesteps the massive overactuation/redundancy problem that normally makes RL exploration in musculoskeletal models intractable, while also cutting how often the policy needs to be queried.
  • Learns human-like sprinting from a minimal task reward in under an hour of training -- framed as a step toward a learnable model of the human motor controller.

PDDL-ART: Autonomous Symbolic Abstraction From Demonstration For Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation Using Vision-Language Models

ArXiv cs.RO
  • Auto-generates task-specific PDDL domain/problem descriptions from a single expert demonstration plus a natural-language task description -- no domain templates, action signatures, or fine-tuning required.
  • Uses a multi-stage correction pipeline (syntactic, semantic, execution) and lets a VLM invoke geometric/temporal reasoning tools to evaluate relational predicates.
  • Achieves 93.3% success on long-horizon manipulation tasks (engine maintenance, household domains) vs. 78.3% for a baseline VLM-based planner.

Drones With Claws Perch on Arctic Icebergs

IEEE Spectrum Robotics
  • Universite de Sherbrooke researchers built the 'Ice Dart,' a 2.65 kg drone with four X-shaped legs, friction shock absorbers, and retractable ice spines (two per foot) that deploy passively, like a cat's claws.
  • Field-tested on Iceland's Fjallsjokull glacier: 100% landing success on slopes up to 58 degrees, at landing speeds up to 3 m/s, in winds up to 30 km/h.
  • Lets researchers perch and monitor glaciers/icebergs for extended periods instead of relying on brief aerial flyover passes; published in IEEE Transactions on Field Robotics.

Is Shipyard Welding the Right First Job for Humanoid Robots?

IEEE Spectrum Robotics
  • Persona AI is targeting linear welding in shipbuilding -- hundreds of kilometers of repetitive, millimeter-precision welds per ship -- as a narrow, high-volume beachhead with no direct humanoid competitors.
  • Partnered with HD Hyundai (world's largest shipbuilder) and POSCO (3rd-largest steel producer) in South Korea, targeting customers who can support hundreds of robots per site.
  • Bipedal form factor is deliberate: shipyards span hundreds of meters with spars to step over, requiring ground-level, overhead, and porthole access; scaling will require tens of thousands of hours of expert demonstration data plus high-fidelity simulation.

How Generalist uses human demonstration data for robot learning

The Robot Report
  • Builds on the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI, from Toyota Research Institute, Columbia, and Stanford): humans operate puppet-like end effectors with GoPro cameras to generate demonstration data for robot foundation models.
  • Remarkably data-efficient -- a tape-dispenser task needed just 2 hours of human demo data plus 4 minutes of on-robot refinement to reach 10/10 consecutive successes.
  • Policies transfer across hardware (Universal Robots arms folding boxes, Flexiv arms repairing vacuums) and exhibit recovery behaviors, like handling dropped objects, that weren't explicitly present in the training data.
