Google reportedly taps AMD to design next-generation TPU — hybrid AI ASIC could integrate on-package CPU cores

Tom's Hardware
  • The rumor is a TPU with general-purpose CPU cores on the same package, targeted specifically at agentic and reinforcement-learning workloads rather than pure training throughput.
  • That design choice is a statement about where the bottleneck went: RL and agent loops interleave short bursts of tensor math with branchy, serial control logic, and shipping that control flow to a host CPU across PCIe wastes most of the accelerator's time.
  • Bringing AMD in as a design partner would also loosen Google's dependence on Broadcom, which has co-designed prior TPU generations.
  • Treat it as an unconfirmed report — but the architectural direction matches what everyone building agent systems keeps running into.

Intel says it will launch new core with Nova Lake on desktop first, not in data center

Tom's Hardware
  • Intel VP Robert Hallock confirms the new core architecture debuts in consumer Nova Lake parts ahead of any Xeon deployment — an inversion of the usual data-center-first cadence.

AVX-512 xor_gen Continues To Look Exciting For Helping Linux RAID With Modern CPUs

Phoronix
  • Google engineer Eric Biggers' AVX-512 rewrite of the kernel's xor_gen() parity routine is now measuring up to a 43% improvement on a Ryzen 9 9950X (Zen 5), up from 41% in the June revision.
  • xor_gen() sits on the hot path for software RAID parity, so the win lands on every write to an md RAID 5/6 array rather than in a synthetic benchmark.
  • The patch series still has not been merged, so the gains are not in a shipping kernel yet — worth tracking if you run software RAID on a recent AMD or Intel part.
  • It is also a clean illustration of how much headroom remains in hand-vectorizing old kernel hot paths for modern ISA extensions.

Linux 7.3 binfmt_misc To Allow BPF Programs To Dynamically Choose Execution Environments

Phoronix
  • binfmt_misc is the kernel facility that routes non-native binaries to an interpreter — Windows EXEs to Wine, JARs to the JVM, foreign-arch binaries to QEMU.
  • The Linux 7.3 merge window extends it so a BPF program can decide the handler dynamically, rather than relying on a static magic-number-to-interpreter table.
  • That makes routing decisions programmable: pick an interpreter based on runtime context, container, or binary contents instead of a fixed registration.
  • Most immediately useful for cross-architecture build and emulation setups, where the right handler often depends on more than the file header.

Intel's Jay Graphics Shader Compiler Now Passing Vulkan CTS With Xe2/Xe3 Hardware

Phoronix
  • Jay, Intel's new shader compiler, now passes the Vulkan Conformance Test Suite on Xe2 and Xe3 hardware — the milestone that separates a research branch from a credible driver path.
  • The effort is led by Alyssa Rosenzweig, who joined Intel last year after contracting for Valve and leading Mesa's Apple Silicon graphics work for Asahi Linux.

3D-printed sound-powered jet engines propel micro drones

Tom's Hardware
  • Researchers built a 3D-printed acoustic resonator that produces thrust when driven at the right ultrasonic frequency — air is pushed out of the nozzle with no moving parts at all.
  • The resulting micro-fliers hover at an effective 12,000 RPM equivalent while being completely silent to human hearing, since the driving frequency sits above the audible band.
  • Thrust is still well below practical levels; this is a proof of concept that the mechanism works, not a flight-ready propulsion system.
  • The no-moving-parts angle is the durable part of the result — it removes bearings and rotor wear from the failure model entirely.

Peer-reviewed study of 443,000 Backblaze hard drives ranks HGST most reliable, Toshiba least

Reddit r/hardware
  • An academic analysis of Backblaze's public drive telemetry — 443,000 drives across 1.66 million drive-years — puts real statistical weight behind numbers usually read as blog-post trivia.
  • The headline finding: Seagate and Toshiba drives fail at roughly twice the rate of Western Digital and HGST units.
  • Peer review matters here because Backblaze's own quarterly posts mix drive models, ages and duty cycles in ways that make naive rate comparisons misleading.
  • Caveat worth keeping: this is one operator's environment and purchasing history, so it reflects how these drives behave in a specific high-density deployment rather than a universal ranking.
