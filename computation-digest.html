Computation Morning Digest

Today's computation news keeps circling the same fault line as yesterday: AI infrastructure's real-world footprint and open source's response to AI-written code. Georgia's largest power utility is acquiring 30 homes via sale or eminent domain for a grid expansion that residents suspect serves a nearby data center, a fresh installment in the AI-power-crunch story. On governance, Debian is now weighing five competing proposals for how to handle AI/LLM contributions, following GCC's committee vote to reject AI-generated code outright. Elsewhere it's a quieter day for the Linux graphics stack: Intel Alchemist media-acceleration patches, AMD's old-GPU format-modifier support, and Valve-backed HDR/AV1 Steam streaming, plus Huawei's shaky bid to upstream a Vulkan driver. On the industry side, Microsoft pushes Copilot further into Classic Outlook while the FTC sues Hims & Hers over health-data sharing with Meta and Snap.
