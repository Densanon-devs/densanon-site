Asahi Linux Now Officially Supports Apple M3 Macs - With Caveats

Phoronix
  • Asahi Linux now officially supports M3, M3 Pro, and M3 Max Macs — the Mac Studio M3 Ultra is the sole exception, still unsupported.
  • GPU acceleration works but isn't yet performant or power-efficient for 3D workloads, and sleep mode plus the HDMI port on M3 MacBooks are dead due to missing Display Controller Panel (DCP) support.
  • The mainline upstream Linux kernel only has bare-bones boot support for M3 so far; full feature parity with Asahi's own tree is expected to land in Linux 7.4 or later.

LLVM Developers Discuss Enabling ClangIR Build By Default

Phoronix
  • ClangIR is a new intermediate representation for Clang built on MLIR, sitting at a higher level than traditional LLVM IR and preserving more C/C++ source-level semantics.
  • Retaining that extra semantic information is what enables better diagnostics and richer static analysis than the current LLVM IR pipeline can offer.
  • After years of development, LLVM developers are now discussing turning ClangIR on by default in builds rather than leaving it opt-in.

Linux 7.3-rc2 Transitions More kmalloc Calls To The Superior kmalloc_obj

Phoronix
  • Linus Torvalds pulled a tree-wide merge for Linux 7.3-rc2 that converts more kmalloc() call sites to the newer kmalloc_obj() family of allocator functions.
  • The change causes significant code churn across the kernel tree since it touches allocation calls throughout many subsystems.
  • kmalloc_obj is considered the superior successor, part of an ongoing kernel-wide push to modernize memory allocation APIs.

Time lords prepare to kick leap seconds into the next millennium

The Register
  • The General Conference on Weights and Measures votes next month on a draft resolution to make UTC continuous starting May 20, 2027, letting it drift from Earth-rotation time (UT1) by up to roughly an hour instead of correcting with leap seconds.
  • The push is driven by a ~30% estimated probability of needing a negative leap second by 2035 — something most digital systems and network time servers were never built to handle since they assume leap seconds only ever add a second.
  • A negative leap second could disrupt telecom networks, power grids, and satellite navigation; the new drift tolerance is projected to avoid leap-second risk for at least 1,000 years. Since 1972, 27 leap seconds have been added to keep UTC within 0.9 seconds of Earth's rotation.

US troops can still be tracked by purchased location data, and Congress wants to know why

The Register
  • Sen. Ron Wyden and Rep. Pat Harrigan, backed by a bipartisan group of 14 lawmakers, are demanding a DoD Inspector General investigation into why commercially purchased location data still exposes military personnel.
  • The Army, Air Force, Navy, Marine Corps, and Special Operations Command all say they now disable advertising IDs on government-issued devices, but some components only did so as recently as July.
  • Lawmakers suspect disabling ad IDs alone is no longer sufficient to stop tracking, and that leakage may also be coming from personnel's and contractors' personal devices via app and ad-SDK data collection.
