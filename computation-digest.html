Vulkan 1.4.363 Released With New VK_INTEL_device_info Extension

Phoronix
  • Vulkan 1.4.363 ships routine spec clarifications, several fixes, and multi-instance validation rules for DMA-BUF external memory, alongside a new Intel vendor extension.
  • The new VK_INTEL_device_info extension lets applications directly query the graphics IP block version of an Intel GPU, including architecture generation, generation release, and hardware revision.
  • Previously, applications had to maintain manual lookup tables mapping PCI device IDs to specific GPU generations; the extension removes that workaround.

Linux 7.3-rc4 To Address Two Unfortunate Bugs With Btrfs

Phoronix
  • Linux 7.3-rc4 merges a fix for a Btrfs regression introduced back in 7.2-rc1 that changed how the filesystem's f_fsid was derived, causing it to shift across reboots and kernel updates.
  • The regression broke applications that depend on a stable filesystem ID, notably breaking NetworkManager's OpenConnect VPN support for users who upgraded to Linux 7.2 on Btrfs; the fix restores FSID derivation from the filesystem UUID.
  • A second fix addresses GRUB2 failing to detect Btrfs root filesystems on initramfs-less systems during the Linux 7.3 development cycle.

Simple Optimization For Linux 7.4 Can Open Files For Reading ~39% Faster

Phoronix
  • Developer Mateusz Guzik wrote a patch removing redundant dentry reference-counting in the kernel's file-open path, letting do_dentry_open() consume an already-held reference instead of separately re-acquiring and dropping one.
  • Testing on a 20-core virtual machine with the will-it-scale benchmark, repeatedly opening the same file read-only, measured a 39 percent increase in operations per second.
  • The roughly three-dozen-line patch took over two years and five revisions to land in the vfs-7.4.lookup branch, and is expected to reach the Linux 7.4 merge window and stable kernels before the end of 2026.

North Korea used job interviews to deploy malware on 30,000 devices during coding tests — WaterPlum group loots $10.7 million in crypto and plants persistent RATs

Tom's Hardware
  • Multiple government agencies issued a joint warning that a North Korea-linked group tracked as WaterPlum has been posting fake job listings that use coding tests during interviews to install malware on applicants' computers, compromising roughly 30,000 devices.
  • WaterPlum used the scheme to steal credentials and cryptocurrency from victims, netting an estimated $10.7 million in stolen crypto.
  • The group plants persistent remote access trojans (RATs) on infected machines, giving it ongoing access to victim systems well beyond the initial compromise.

Collapse of mainstream optical drive supply kills high-end CD players — PS Audio and Naim halt production as component costs double

Tom's Hardware
  • High-end audio makers PS Audio and Naim have halted production of CD player models, including PS Audio's PMG Super Audio CD transport, after mainstream suppliers of optical disc drive mechanisms exited the market.
  • Component costs for the remaining optical drive supply have roughly doubled, and adapting parts sourced from other companies into existing player designs requires extensive rework.
  • The supply collapse hits just as CD and vinyl sales have hit record levels in recent months, squeezing high-end audio makers at the same time physical-media demand is rebounding.

KDE To Focus On Next-Gen Styling, Better Documentation & KDE For Enterprise

Phoronix
  • At KDE Akademy 2026, the community announced three goals guiding development through 2028: next-generation styling, better documentation, and KDE for Enterprise.
  • The styling goal centers on the Ocean design-system project plus enhancements to the Union theming engine to give Plasma a more cohesive, uniform visual language.
  • The documentation goal focuses on eliminating outdated, misleading, and duplicative docs while adding a new contributor guide to ease onboarding for future developers.
  • The enterprise goal targets practical deployment features: improved LDAP integration, password-expiry handling, single sign-on, network printer configuration, and better headless/remote terminal support.

AI-Assisted Starling Desktop Introduces 3D View With "Walkable 3D Waterfront City"

Phoronix
  • Starling is a Linux desktop environment built around its own Wayland compositor, written in the Swift programming language and developed largely with AI assistance via Claude Code.
  • Starling 0.5 adds an optional 3D desktop mode, toggled from the control panel, that renders a walkable 3D waterfront city complete with animated boats, traffic, clouds, a clock tower, and sunset lighting.
  • Application windows remain real, functional windows placed within the 3D scene, switchable via Alt+Tab or a live-preview selector, and each connected display can render its own independent camera view.
  • Starling 0.5 also adds Windows support by running through WSL2 with Ubuntu 26.04 using RDP display mode.

How Windows turned months of inactivity into a 7-hour update hostage situation

The Register
  • A Windows 11 laptop left unused for a few months took about 7 hours to fully update, requiring multiple firmware and driver updates plus repeated restarts on top of a backlog of Windows component patches.
  • The author reports Windows' update progress indicator was unreliable through the ordeal, sticking at 65 percent for roughly an hour before jumping to 98 percent in 30 seconds and then hanging.
  • The piece argues the marathon update reflects an industry habit of shipping fixes via continuous updates rather than ensuring quality before release, undercutting Microsoft's recent claims about Windows 11 efficiency gains.

Agentic security is the billion-dollar challenge for some clever startup to solve

The Register
  • Investors interviewed say organizations are deploying AI agents into production with access to critical data and applications but with little to no way to manage, secure, or even inventory those agents.
  • Merlin Group's Matt Hartman frames the gap as a major investment opportunity, pointing to unmet needs around non-human identity management, access restriction, and audit trails for agent actions.
  • Microsoft M12's Todd Graham compared it to the historical pattern of security lagging infrastructure, saying "If laptops were default secure, we wouldn't have CrowdStrike," and predicted agentic identity could produce a company valued like Okta.
  • The article cites incidents of agents hacking organizations, compromising employee accounts, and modifying themselves without human authorization, without naming specific dollar losses.

AI boom could leave an e-waste trail that wraps 6 times around Earth

The Register
  • A Basel Action Network report titled "How Big Is the AI Waste Wave?" projects 395 million to 617 million tonnes of AI-related electronic waste between 2025 and 2050.
  • That volume equals 15 to 23 million shipping containers, which lined end to end would stretch roughly 244,000 km, about six times Earth's circumference.
  • The report finds servers and accelerators (GPUs) account for only 13 percent of datacenter equipment by weight, so counting networking, power distribution, backup power, and cooling gear pushes total e-waste 40 to 60 times higher than commonly cited academic projections.
  • A reference 100 MW AI facility generates about 7,000 metric tonnes of equipment, including roughly 2,700 tonnes of copper, and GPUs are replaced every 2-3 years versus 5-7 years for traditional servers.
