US frontier AI companies warn authorities over sophisticated distillation attacks — China warns of 'countermeasures' if America tries to constrain domestic AI models

Tom's Hardware
  • US frontier AI labs are lobbying the government over 'distillation attacks,' where overseas actors extract knowledge from proprietary frontier models to cheaply train competing ones.
  • The concern centers on rivals closing the capability gap through distillation without matching the compute investment behind the original models.
  • China has signaled it would respond with 'countermeasures' if the US moves to legally restrict how its domestic models can be accessed or queried.

Modder gets Nvidia's DLSS 5 working in a web browser using WebGPU — 147MB browser port runs on non-Nvidia GPUs and macOS but takes two seconds per render

Tom's Hardware
  • A developer ported Nvidia's DLSS 5 neural upscaler to run entirely in-browser via WebGPU, sidestepping Nvidia's usual driver and hardware lock-in.
  • The port weighs in at 147MB and runs on non-Nvidia GPUs and macOS, though each frame currently takes about two seconds to render, far from real-time.
  • It's a proof-of-concept showing WebGPU compute shaders can host a proprietary neural rendering model outside its vendor's native stack.

ASML snubs Elon Musk-backed particle accelerator chipmaking tech — firm doubles down on 1,000W laser-produced plasma systems for chipmaking tools

Tom's Hardware
  • ASML is passing on free-electron-laser particle-accelerator EUV light sources, an approach tied to an Elon Musk-backed venture, in favor of scaling its existing laser-produced plasma technology.
  • The company is pushing its LPP EUV sources toward 1,000W of output power to boost scanner throughput without adopting a fundamentally new light-source architecture.
  • The decision reinforces ASML's incumbent approach to EUV lithography, betting on incremental LPP scaling over the alternative FEL path's engineering risk.

Intel Appears To End Its Bug Bounty Program

Phoronix

Rust Issues Warning Over Key Developers Being Targeted For Compromise

Phoronix
  • The Rust security response working group and the crates.io team issued a joint warning that key Rust developers are being targeted in a coordinated compromise attempt.
  • The warning signals concern about supply-chain risk to the Rust toolchain or crates.io registry if a maintainer's credentials or commit access were compromised.
  • It follows a pattern seen in other major open-source ecosystems, such as npm and PyPI, where attackers target maintainer accounts as a high-leverage supply-chain entry point.
