Computation Morning Digest

Today's digest is bookended by supply and legitimacy questions in hardware: Pine64 is freezing all Linux-capable device production through mid-2027 on a DRAM shortage, while Seasonic's first 80 Plus Ruby-certified ATX PSU shows efficiency engineering pushing forward regardless. On the chip-industry side, SMIC's record $3B quarter is a direct product of US export sanctions walling off Chinese AI compute demand into a captive domestic foundry market, and a Tom's Hardware HPC deep-dive argues the TOP500-style supercomputer rankings race itself is losing meaning now that the largest clusters are privately held and skip the HPL benchmark entirely. The open-source stack keeps grinding forward in parallel — Btrfs picks up 3x-5x performance wins for the Linux 7.3 merge window, Mesa and X.Org Server both ship maturity releases (the latter its first major feature release in five years), and Go 1.27 finally closes the generic-methods gap. A community-reverse-engineered Linux driver for a $700 PreSonus audio interface rounds out the day as a reminder that vendor driver support is still often optional, not guaranteed.

Hardware & Chips

Seasonic unveils world's first 80 Plus Ruby ATX power supply — Prime Enterprise RX-1600 delivers 1600W with up to 95.4% efficiency

Tom's Hardware
  • Seasonic's Prime Enterprise RX-1600 is the first ATX-form-factor PSU to earn 80 Plus Ruby certification, the efficiency tier above Titanium.
  • The 1600W unit hits up to 95.4% efficiency across the 20-50% load range on standard 115V input.
  • It ships in a standard ATX form factor rather than a proprietary server chassis, so it can slot into existing high-end enthusiast or workstation builds.

Pine64 halts all Linux hardware manufacturing through at least mid-2027 due to shortages — memory crunch forces open-source maker to freeze SBCs, tablets, and phones

Tom's Hardware
  • Pine64 is pausing production of all Linux-capable hardware — SBCs, tablets, and phones — through at least mid-2027 due to a memory (DRAM) shortage.
  • Microcontroller-based products like the PineTime smartwatch, PineVoice smart speaker, and Pinecil soldering iron are unaffected since they don't depend on the same memory supply.
  • The halt is a concrete example of the current memory crunch hitting small open-source hardware makers hardest, since they lack the volume purchasing leverage of larger OEMs.

Industry & HPC

SMIC posts record $3B quarter and hikes wafer prices — US sanctions hand Chinese foundry a captive AI market

Tom's Hardware
  • SMIC, China's largest chip foundry, posted its first-ever $3 billion quarter, with revenue up 36.1% year-over-year and net profit nearly tripling to $479.2 million.
  • The foundry is raising wafer prices into what the report frames as a shortage rather than competing on price.
  • US export sanctions that wall off Chinese firms from foreign foundries are credited with handing SMIC a captive domestic AI-chip customer base with little alternative sourcing.

The supercomputer race no longer means what it used to, as rankings lose relevance in the AI era — as privately held compute clusters are built, running HPL becomes a distraction

Tom's Hardware
  • Tom's Hardware interviewed HPC experts, including the deputy head of high-performance computing at GWDG, on the current state of the supercomputer rankings race.
  • The piece argues that public TOP500-style rankings are losing relevance as the largest compute clusters are now privately held by hyperscalers and AI labs that don't run the HPL benchmark used to rank public systems.
  • Running HPL purely to chase a leaderboard spot is increasingly viewed as a distraction from a cluster's actual AI workloads.
