A breakthrough in magnetism could make AI chips use dramatically less energy

ScienceDaily
  • Brown University researchers eliminated the "dead layer" problem that makes multiferroic bismuth ferrite lose its electrical and magnetic behavior when made extremely thin. They grew the film on dysprosium scandate, whose slightly smaller crystal structure compresses it, and added a second metallic layer to counteract unwanted internal electric fields.
  • The films kept ferroelectric and magnetic properties down to a few angstroms and also showed altermagnetism (published in Nature Materials). The technology still needs development before commercial chip use.

Europe's Digital Wallets May Be More Traceable Than Intended

IEEE Spectrum
  • EU digital identity wallets must be unlinkable under the European Digital Identity Regulation, but verifiers record identifiers and signatures that two verifiers could pool to recognize the same person, as Johannes Schönrich notes. Presenting multiple credential versions fails if the issuer can connect them to one person, per Anna Lysyanskaya.
  • Cryptographers recommend zero-knowledge proofs or anonymous credentials such as the BBS signature scheme, and a Google team showed ECDSA-based zero-knowledge proofs run efficiently on smartphones. The Commission's June 2026 GitHub post says they are not yet mandatory.

Longstanding physics puzzle behind radiation damage in electronics solved

r/hardware
  • NC State researchers (Ramón Collazo, Cristyan Quiñones) explain single-event burnout in aluminum nitride diodes: heavy ions create charge carriers, the applied voltage separates them into a localized field, and at a critical level this triggers an electron avalanche releasing energy in a small area. Burnout thresholds fall as ion linear energy transfer rises.
  • AlN withstood fields close to an order of magnitude higher than other common semiconductors, and the analytical model fit data across several material systems. The paper appeared in IEEE Transactions on Nuclear Science on Nov. 5, 2026.

Court denies motion to dismiss DRAM price-fixing class action

r/hardware
  • Judge Noël Wise of the Northern District of California denied in full the motion by Samsung, Micron and SK hynix to dismiss a class action alleging they conspired to limit DRAM supply. The court found a plausible Sherman Act Section 1 agreement and rejected the argument that the conduct was just a response to AI demand.
  • The complaint alleges the three firms held over 91% of DRAM revenue and that conventional DRAM prices rose 697% over 18 months. These are allegations only, and defendants' answers are due December 7, 2026.
