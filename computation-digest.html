45 years later, earliest DOS source code transcribed from a stack of old printouts found in a garage

Tom's Hardware
  • 86-DOS 1.00 source was recovered from physical printouts found in a garage, manually transcribed, and open-sourced to mark the codebase's 45th anniversary — this is the earliest known DOS code to be made publicly available.
  • The transcription-from-printout methodology is notable: no digital backup existed, making the recovery entirely manual, and the result directly documents the x86 software foundation that eventually became MS-DOS.

Industry & HPC

FCC votes to ban all Chinese labs from certifying electronics sold in the US due to national security concerns

Tom's Hardware
  • 75% of US-bound electronics are currently tested at Chinese certification facilities — this ruling forces a full re-routing of EMC/RF compliance testing to US or approved third-country labs, adding cost and lead time across the entire consumer electronics supply chain.
  • The impact hits everyone from peripheral makers to GPU board partners; expect SKU rationalization and pricing pressure on hardware that previously relied on fast, cheap China-based certification turnaround.
  • This is a structural supply-chain shift, not a tariff — it rewrites where certification infrastructure needs to exist, with multi-year ripple effects for hardware manufacturers and importers.
