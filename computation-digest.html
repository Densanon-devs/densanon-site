Kyoto University builds transistor that survives 600C temperatures, compatible with standard fabs — Standard ion implantation and bottom-gate design fix leakage and voltage drift

Tom's Hardware
  • Kyoto University demonstrated a silicon carbide (SiC) transistor that operates reliably at 600C (873K), built with standard ion implantation rather than an exotic process.
  • A bottom-gate design combined with standard ion implantation fixes the leakage current and voltage drift that normally plague SiC devices at extreme temperatures.
  • Compatibility with standard fabs means this could reach practical use in engines, deep drilling, or spacecraft electronics without a bespoke process line.

Undersea cable to Antarctica through Drake Passage is viable, researchers find — 1,600km route to Chile could spell an end to research data leaving in 'suitcases full of hard drives'

Tom's Hardware
  • Researchers found a 1,600km submarine cable route across the Drake Passage to Chile is technically buildable, giving Antarctic research stations a real path to network connectivity.
  • Today, Antarctic research data still leaves the continent physically, ferried out by ship and plane in suitcases full of hard drives -- a cable would replace that logistics chain with a direct link.
  • Given the Drake Passage's seas and seafloor geology, the finding is notable mainly as a feasibility result, not a signal that a build is imminent.

Linux 7.3 Better Protects Against Buggy EFI Firmware Taking Down The Rest Of User-Space

Phoronix
  • Linux 7.3's EFI subsystem now enforces a timeout on EFI runtime service calls instead of letting a hang wait indefinitely.
  • Closes a real reliability hole: buggy vendor firmware could previously wedge the whole machine by hanging a single EFI call, taking the rest of user-space down with it.

Nintendo takes down over 400 GitHub repositories for Switch emulators in one go — Relentless DMCA strikes tied to piracy concerns over illegal cryptographic keys

Tom's Hardware
  • Nintendo filed DMCA takedowns against 7 master repositories and their forks, wiping 401 GitHub repos for Switch emulators in a single day.
  • Most targets were Yuzu forks such as Suyu; the strikes are tied to distribution of the cryptographic keys needed to decrypt Switch content, not just the emulator code itself.
  • Shows the legal pressure on emulation projects has shifted toward anything downstream of leaked keys, not only the original codebase.
